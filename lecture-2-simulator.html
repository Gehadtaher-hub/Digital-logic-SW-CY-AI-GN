Lecture 2: Logic Gates

A logic gate is an electronic decision-making circuit. It receives binary inputs, where 0 represents LOW and 1 represents HIGH, then produces one binary output.

Example and teaching note

Start by reading the output rule. Then test every possible input combination in the simulator.

Binary Signals and Gate Notation

Digital circuits use only two stable states. Inputs are usually named A and B, and the output is named Y. A small circle, called an inversion bubble, means NOT.

Example and teaching note

Example: if A = 1, then NOT A = 0. The bar in Y = A̅ also means inversion.

Slide 3 of 12

NOT Gate

The NOT gate has one input. It reverses the input, so Y = A̅. A LOW input becomes HIGH, and a HIGH input becomes LOW.

Example and teaching note

Use NOT when a circuit must react to the opposite condition, such as turning on an alarm when a safety signal is absent.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 4 of 12

AND Gate

The AND gate produces 1 only when all inputs are 1. Its Boolean expression is Y = A · B. Any zero input forces the output to zero.

Example and teaching note

Think of two series switches. Current flows only when switch A and switch B are both closed.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 5 of 12

OR Gate

The OR gate produces 1 when at least one input is 1. Its Boolean expression is Y = A + B. The output is zero only when all inputs are zero.

Example and teaching note

Use OR when any one of several conditions is enough, such as alarm = smoke OR heat.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 6 of 12

NAND Gate

NAND means NOT-AND. Its output is the inverse of AND: Y = (A · B)̅. It is zero only for A = 1 and B = 1.

Example and teaching note

NAND is a universal gate because NOT, AND, OR, and any Boolean function can be built using NAND gates only.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 7 of 12

NOR Gate

NOR means NOT-OR. Its output is the inverse of OR: Y = (A + B)̅. It is one only when both inputs are zero.

Example and teaching note

NOR is also universal. A NOR gate with its two inputs tied together behaves as a NOT gate.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 8 of 12

XOR Gate

XOR is the exclusive-OR gate. For two inputs, Y = A ⊕ B is 1 when the inputs are different.

Example and teaching note

XOR is used in sum circuits and inequality detectors. In a half adder, Sum = A ⊕ B.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 9 of 12

XNOR Gate

XNOR is the inverse of XOR. Y = (A ⊕ B)̅ is 1 when the inputs are equal.

Example and teaching note

XNOR is an equality detector, so it can compare two bits and produce 1 when they match.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 10 of 12

Gate Families Compared

AND checks whether all conditions are true. OR checks whether any condition is true. XOR checks whether inputs differ. NAND, NOR, and XNOR invert those results.

Example and teaching note

Compare gate truth tables row by row. Focus on the unique row or pattern that produces 1.

Standard gate symbol

Slide 11 of 12

Universal-Gate Conversions

NAND-only NOT: connect the same signal to both NAND inputs. NAND-only AND: NAND first, then invert its output with another NAND. Similar constructions can create OR.

Example and teaching note

Universal gates simplify manufacturing because one gate type can implement a complete circuit.

Applications and Review

NOT provides inversion, AND provides permission, OR combines alternatives, XOR detects difference, and XNOR detects equality. NAND and NOR can implement complete systems.
